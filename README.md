# Diabetes\_Progression

## Overview



This project investigates how well baseline physical and biochemical measurements can predict one-year diabetes progression using statistical modeling.



This study uses a dataset of 442 patients and applies multiple linear regression to control whether combining different measures like Body Mass Index (BMI), blood pressure, and serum triglycerides have their predictive performance compared to using an individual indicator  like BMI alone.



## Objectives



Which patient characteristics are associated with diabetes progression, what do the regression models tell us, and what conclusions can reasonably be drawn from the data? 



## Methodology



The analysis follows a structured statistical workflow:



1\. *Data Preparation — inspect, clean, and organize the dataset.

2\. Exploratory Data Analysis — investigate distributions, relationships, and potential patterns.

3\. Regression Modeling— apply multiple linear regression using relevant physical and biochemical predictors.

4\. Model Evaluation— assess predictive accuracy and the contribution of individual variables.

5\. Interpretation — distinguish observed statistical associations from causal relationships.

6\. Executive Summary— translate the statistical findings into practical population-level insights.



## Key Findings



The analysis demonstrates that combining BMI, blood pressure, and serum triglycerides can provide better predictive information about our sample than relying on a single indicator alone.



However, predictive associations should not automatically be interpreted as causal effects. The model may be affected by **unmeasured confounding variables, individual variability, measurement uncertainty, and other limitations** inherent in observational data.



## Interpretation



These models can help indicating a level of variance in the progression, but they are not intended to replace personal clinical judgment. Predictions must be interpreted alongside other relevant patient information and with  consideration of model uncertainty.



## Dataset



On 442 patients we try to predict the One-year diabetes progression using Physical and biochemical baseline measurements such as BMI, blood pressure, and serum triglycerides.
